# Jorge Aviles

**Software Engineering · Cloud & Data Systems · Applied Research**

I build cloud applications, data platforms, and AI integrations for complex business workflows. My background spans business research, finance, and the legal industry, connecting practical software development with analytical questions about how organizations operate and make decisions.

### What I work on

- **Applications** — C#, ASP.NET Core, and Blazor, with an emphasis on clear interfaces and reliable behavior.
- **Data platforms** — SQL, Python, PySpark, Azure Synapse Analytics, and Cosmos DB.
- **Applied AI** — Azure AI Foundry, document retrieval, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and Model Context Protocol (MCP) integrations.
- **Automation** — Tools that connect systems, reduce repetitive work, and make information easier to use.

### Research background

I spent nearly three years as a research assistant at **CENTRUM Católica Graduate Business School, Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú (PUCP)** in Lima, Peru, supporting faculty research in:

- **Profitability forecasting** — Machine learning approaches to predicting cattle-ranch profitability and evaluating business methodology.
- **Consumer choice and pricing** — Market research on spending habits, choice-based conjoint analysis, and price modeling.

I also supported coordination for the school's accreditation activities.

**Selected research contribution:** Research assistance acknowledged as *J. Avilés* in Anand N. Asthana's [*Profitability Prediction in Cattle Ranches in Latin America: A Machine Learning Approach*](https://centrumthink.pucp.edu.pe/articulosacademicos/profitability-prediction-in-catlle-ranches-in-latin-america-a-machine-learning-approach/), *Global Veterinaria*, 13(4), 473–495 (2014). The study examines vector space models and kernel ridge regression for profitability prediction.

### Engineering approach

Understand the business problem, examine the data and assumptions, and build systems whose behavior can be tested and explained. I value clear interfaces, reliable data pipelines, careful handling of sensitive information, and small, durable improvements to working software.

Much of my current engineering work lives in private repositories. My public repositories include forks reflecting my interests in developer tools, automation, and audio technology.
